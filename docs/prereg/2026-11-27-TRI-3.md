# Pre-registration: third numeric test of batch TRI (deduplication at the A5 points)

Registered 2026-11-27 (session 15), after the second registered test (`docs/prereg/2026-11-27-TRI-2.md`) was run and
before the program below has been run. Both earlier outputs are kept unchanged
(`numerics/results-tri-checks-2026-11-27.txt`, `numerics/results-tri-checks2-2026-11-27.txt`; both OVERALL: FAIL).

**What was known at registration: everything from both runs and from a diagnostic.**
- TRI-2: T1 PASS at the nine values a ≠ ±a* (including 0.05 and 0.5, the first run's other failures); T2, K1, K2
  PASS. T1 FAIL at a = a* and at a = −a* only: 9 solutions each, 7 of them with `|q₄| < 1e−12` where criterion (ii)
  asks for exactly 2; all 7 with `q₆ = ∓0.0147313912747`, the predicted `−sign(a)/(48√2)`.
- The diagnostic `numerics/review/tri_diag_a5_dedup.py` (not registered; output
  `numerics/results-tri-diag-a5-dedup-2026-11-27.txt`) ran the registered search at a = a\*. The 7 solutions lie
  within 2.9e−15 of the two predicted A5 points: 2 at `(θ, −θ)` and 5 at `(−θ, θ)`, cos θ = −2a\*. The two other
  solutions are the matching-sign points `(±π/2, ±π/2)`, with `q₄ = −0.08839 = −1/(32a*)`. The Jacobian determinant
  of the search system is about 1e−30 at the copies, so the root is degenerate. Grouped at tolerance 1e−8, the
  solutions form 4 groups with spread at most 5.5e−15. The search merged solutions only at distance 1e−15, so the
  copies survived.

So the TRI-2 failure is a defect of the program's deduplication, not a counterexample to K1 or K3: every copy lies at
a predicted A5 point and carries the predicted sextic. The outcome rule of TRI-2 reads "a FAIL of T1 (i) or (ii) is a
counterexample to K1 or K3 and stops the batch". The first registration reads "stops the batch until it is
explained". The lead applies the first registration's wording, since the second inherits its protocol. This is a
reading of a registered rule, recorded here and in the session log for the owner to overrule. This test is not
independent; it decides whether the deduplication was the only cause.

## Protocol (fixed now)

Everything is as in TRI-2: the values of a, criteria T1 (i) to (iv), T2, K1 and K2 with the same tolerances, the
first program's functions, and TRI-2's two changes (400 Newton steps, interpolation step 5e−4). The third program
loads the second and refuses to run unless its SHA-256 is the registered
4953f187d6aa17e871420bb10fdaf3f6e5da3878f159b539a29722f6b62126f1; the second checks the first in turn. Changes:

1. In the search, two accepted solutions are the same solution when `|sin((x − x′)/2)| + |sin((y − y′)/2)| < 1e−8`
   (it was 1e−15). Everything else in the search is as in TRI-2.
2. New criterion **D**, so that the larger tolerance cannot hide distinct solutions. For every registered a, every
   merged solution lies within 1e−12 of the solution it was merged into, and any two distinct solutions are at
   distance at least 1e−3.

Overall PASS iff the first program's OVERALL line is PASS and D is PASS for every a.

**Outcomes and what follows, fixed now.**
- PASS: the earlier failures were of resolution and of deduplication. The statements of batch TRI are frozen.
- A FAIL of T1 (i) or (ii) is a counterexample to K1 or K3 and stops the batch until it is explained.
- A FAIL of D at some a means the solutions there are not separated at the scale of the tolerance. That is a defect
  of the test, to be explained before the freeze.
- Any other FAIL is a defect, to be explained before the freeze.

**What the test cannot show.** As in the first registration: a search from finitely many starts does not prove that
all points were found, and floating-point values are not enclosures.

**Before registration:** the program was compiled, its search was run once on a 2 × 2 grid at a = 0.3, and its
final summary was run once with the first program's `main` replaced by a stub. This was a smoke test of the code
path. It is not a result, and its output is not kept.
